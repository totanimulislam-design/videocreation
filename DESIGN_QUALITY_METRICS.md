# DESIGN_QUALITY_METRICS.md

# Professional Graphic Design Quality Evaluation Framework

> **Measure Design Quality. Identify Weaknesses. Refine Every Composition.**

This document defines the quality measurement, evaluation, scoring, critique, and refinement standards for all graphic designs generated by this platform.

The AI must use this framework to evaluate its generated designs before considering them complete.

The purpose is to establish a measurable quality control system inspired by professional graphic design studios, advertising agencies, creative directors, and print production workflows.

---

# 1. CORE OBJECTIVE

A design must not be considered successful simply because it renders correctly.

Technical correctness and visual quality are two different things.

A design can be technically valid but visually poor.

For example:

- All elements may render correctly, but the composition may be generic.
- Typography may be visible, but the hierarchy may be weak.
- Colors may be attractive individually, but the palette may lack harmony.
- Images may be high-resolution, but their placement may be ineffective.
- The layout may be aligned, but the overall design may feel unbalanced.

Therefore, every generated design must undergo two independent evaluation processes:

**A. Technical Quality Evaluation**

Checks whether the design is structurally correct, editable, readable, and exportable.

**B. Artistic Quality Evaluation**

Checks whether the design demonstrates professional creativity, visual sophistication, composition, and artistic direction.

Both are mandatory.

# 2. QUALITY SCORING SYSTEM

Use a weighted scoring system with a maximum score of 100 points.

| Evaluation Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Creative Direction & Originality | 12 |
| Composition & Visual Balance | 15 |
| Visual Hierarchy | 12 |
| Typography | 14 |
| Color Theory & Harmony | 10 |
| Spacing & Alignment | 8 |
| Imagery & Graphic Elements | 8 |
| Depth, Effects & Visual Refinement | 6 |
| Communication & Readability | 8 |
| Technical & Production Quality | 7 |
| **TOTAL** | **100** |

Every category must receive a score according to its own criteria.

Do not assign high scores simply because the design looks attractive at first glance.

Evaluate the actual rendered output.

## Overall Quality Levels

| Score | Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 95–100 | Exceptional | Outstanding professional execution |
| 90–94 | Excellent | Highly refined professional design |
| 85–89 | Professional | Strong and production-ready |
| 80–84 | Good | Effective with minor improvements |
| 70–79 | Acceptable | Needs noticeable refinement |
| 60–69 | Weak | Significant design problems |
| 40–59 | Poor | Major creative and visual weaknesses |
| 0–39 | Failed | Unacceptable output |

### Minimum Acceptance Standard

- Target score: 90 or above.
- Minimum delivery score: 85.
- Below 85: Mandatory revision.
- Below 70: Major redesign recommended.

A score of 85 does not automatically authorize delivery if a critical technical or content requirement fails.

# 3. DETAILED EVALUATION CRITERIA

## 3.1 Creative Direction & Originality — 12 Points

Evaluate whether the design has a distinctive and appropriate creative identity.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Clear creative concept | 3 |
| Appropriate artistic direction | 2 |
| Original composition approach | 3 |
| Consistent visual language | 2 |
| Appropriate visual personality | 2 |
| **Total** | **12** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Does the design have a clear creative concept?
- Is the selected style appropriate for the subject?
- Does it look intentionally art-directed?
- Is the composition distinctive?
- Does it avoid generic template aesthetics?
- Are visual elements consistent with the selected style?

### Deduction Rules

Deduct points for:

- Generic template-like composition.
- Random decorative elements.
- Inconsistent visual language.
- Unnecessary stylistic combinations.
- Lack of creative identity.

A design must not receive a high originality score simply because it uses unusual colors or unusual shapes.

Originality must be supported by meaningful creative decisions.

## 3.2 Composition & Visual Balance — 15 Points

Evaluate the overall arrangement of visual elements.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Overall composition | 4 |
| Visual balance | 3 |
| Focal point placement | 3 |
| Visual flow | 2 |
| Negative space | 2 |
| Spatial relationships | 1 |
| **Total** | **15** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Is the composition visually compelling?
- Is the focal point positioned effectively?
- Does the design have a balanced distribution of visual weight?
- Is the viewer's eye guided naturally?
- Is negative space used effectively?
- Are all elements spatially related?

### Critical Problems

- Accidental element collisions.
- Unbalanced visual weight.
- Poorly positioned focal points.
- Excessive empty or crowded areas.
- Confusing visual flow.

Intentional asymmetry and overlapping artwork must not be penalized when they strengthen the composition.

## 3.3 Visual Hierarchy — 12 Points

Evaluate how effectively the design communicates information priorities.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Primary focal point | 3 |
| Headline prominence | 3 |
| Secondary information hierarchy | 2 |
| Contrast between information levels | 2 |
| Overall information flow | 2 |
| **Total** | **12** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Can the viewer identify the main message quickly?
- Is the headline visually prominent?
- Are supporting details appropriately subordinate?
- Is the hierarchy understandable?
- Are important elements competing unnecessarily?

### Critical Problems

- Every element appears equally important.
- Supporting text competes with the headline.
- Important information is visually hidden.
- Decorative elements dominate the actual message.

## 3.4 Typography — 14 Points

Typography must receive special attention because it strongly influences professional design quality.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Typeface selection | 2 |
| Font pairing | 2 |
| Typographic hierarchy | 3 |
| Spacing and line height | 2 |
| Readability | 2 |
| Typography composition | 2 |
| Language rendering | 1 |
| **Total** | **14** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Are the selected fonts appropriate?
- Do the typefaces work well together?
- Is the headline visually effective?
- Is body text readable?
- Are line breaks intentional?
- Is the line spacing comfortable?
- Are font weights used intelligently?
- Are Bengali and Arabic characters rendered correctly?

### Critical Problems

- Missing glyphs.
- Incorrect Bengali conjunct rendering.
- Incorrect Arabic shaping.
- Text overflow.
- Clipped headlines.
- Unreadable text.
- Unexpected font substitution.

Any essential text rendering failure must trigger technical rejection regardless of the total score.

## 3.5 Color Theory & Harmony — 10 Points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Palette selection | 3 |
| Color harmony | 2 |
| Contrast | 2 |
| Accent color distribution | 1 |
| Background and foreground relationships | 2 |
| **Total** | **10** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Does the palette support the creative direction?
- Are colors visually harmonious?
- Is the accent color used appropriately?
- Is text contrast sufficient?
- Does the background support the content?
- Are colors consistent throughout the design?

### Critical Problems

- Poor foreground/background contrast.
- Random color combinations.
- Excessive accent colors.
- Inconsistent color usage.
- Unnecessary gradient effects.

## 3.6 Spacing & Alignment — 8 Points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Margin consistency | 2 |
| Element alignment | 2 |
| Spacing relationships | 2 |
| Padding and grouping | 1 |
| Optical alignment | 1 |
| **Total** | **8** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Are elements aligned intentionally?
- Are margins consistent?
- Is spacing proportional?
- Are related elements grouped?
- Is there sufficient separation between unrelated content?
- Are optical adjustments appropriate?

Do not require identical spacing everywhere. Different visual relationships may require different spacing values.

## 3.7 Imagery & Graphic Elements — 8 Points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Image relevance | 2 |
| Image quality | 1 |
| Image placement and cropping | 2 |
| Illustration consistency | 1 |
| Shape and graphic integration | 2 |
| **Total** | **8** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Are images relevant to the message?
- Do they support the composition?
- Are images appropriately cropped?
- Are illustrations visually consistent?
- Are shapes integrated meaningfully?
- Are graphic elements contributing to the design?

A design does not require photographs or illustrations to achieve a high score. Typography-led and minimalist designs should be evaluated according to their intended creative direction.

## 3.8 Depth, Effects & Visual Refinement — 6 Points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Layering | 2 |
| Effects discipline | 1 |
| Visual depth | 1 |
| Surface refinement | 1 |
| Overall finish | 1 |
| **Total** | **6** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Are layers organized effectively?
- Are effects appropriate?
- Does depth improve the composition?
- Are textures and shadows controlled?
- Does the final artwork feel refined?

Do not reward excessive effects.

A clean, flat, minimalist design can receive full marks.

## 3.9 Communication & Readability — 8 Points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Message clarity | 3 |
| Information accuracy | 2 |
| Audience suitability | 1 |
| Reading comfort | 1 |
| Call-to-action effectiveness | 1 |
| **Total** | **8** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Does the design communicate its purpose?
- Is the message understandable?
- Is all supplied information preserved?
- Is the design appropriate for its audience?
- Can the viewer read the essential content?
- Is the call-to-action visible when required?

Never invent names, addresses, phone numbers, dates, prices, or other factual information.

## 3.10 Technical & Production Quality — 7 Points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Document dimensions | 1 |
| Structural validity | 1 |
| Element editability | 1 |
| Asset and font availability | 1 |
| Rendering consistency | 1 |
| Export quality | 1 |
| Print/digital specifications | 1 |
| **Total** | **7** |

### Evaluation Questions

- Is the document valid?
- Are dimensions correct?
- Are elements editable?
- Are all assets available?
- Does the preview match the export?
- Is the output resolution sufficient?
- Are print requirements satisfied where applicable?

# 4. CRITICAL FAILURE CONDITIONS

Certain problems must trigger rejection regardless of the total score.

These are mandatory quality gates.

## Content Failure

- Missing essential information.
- Incorrect user-provided information.
- Unintended text substitution.
- Broken characters.
- Unreadable essential text.

## Rendering Failure

- Missing images.
- Missing fonts.
- Broken SVG paths.
- Unexpected clipping.
- Major rendering artifacts.
- Incorrect object positioning.

## Layout Failure

- Essential elements outside the document.
- Accidental overlapping that obscures content.
- Unusable text sizes.
- Major composition breakage.

## Export Failure

- Corrupted output.
- Incorrect document dimensions.
- Missing essential content.
- Unsupported or broken assets.
- Unacceptable output quality.

### Critical Gate Rule

```text
IF critical_failure_exists:
    STATUS = REJECTED
    DELIVERY_ALLOWED = FALSE
    REVISION_REQUIRED = TRUE
```

An artistic design can intentionally use unusual cropping, overlapping, or asymmetry. Only unintended or harmful failures should trigger rejection.

# 5. OBJECTIVE METRICS VS SUBJECTIVE EVALUATION

Not every aspect of graphic design can be measured mathematically.

The system must separate measurable technical properties from subjective artistic judgment.

## 5.1 Objective Metrics

Use deterministic algorithms for:

- Document dimensions.
- Element bounds.
- Text overflow.
- Asset availability.
- Font availability.
- Object collisions.
- Safe-area violations.
- Alignment deviation.
- Spacing consistency.
- Contrast ratios.
- Export resolution.
- SVG validity.

These should be verified through actual programmatic checks.

## 5.2 Subjective Metrics

Use AI-assisted visual evaluation for:

- Creative originality.
- Artistic direction.
- Visual sophistication.
- Composition quality.
- Visual rhythm.
- Aesthetic refinement.
- Image integration.
- Design personality.
- Overall professional appearance.

These require inspection of the actual rendered design.

Do not pretend that subjective design quality can be determined perfectly through geometry or numerical calculations alone.

# 7. AI DESIGN CRITIC SYSTEM

The AI Design Critic must act as an independent creative reviewer.

Its responsibility is to identify weaknesses, not simply approve the generated design.

## Critic Responsibilities

1. Inspect the rendered artwork.
2. Understand the original design brief.
3. Compare the output with the intended creative direction.
4. Evaluate visual hierarchy.
5. Evaluate composition.
6. Evaluate typography.
7. Evaluate color harmony.
8. Evaluate imagery.
9. Evaluate visual balance.
10. Identify generic design patterns.
11. Identify unnecessary decoration.
12. Suggest specific improvements.

## Critic Output Schema

```typescript
interface DesignEvaluation {
  totalScore: number;

  categoryScores: {
    creativeDirection: number;
    composition: number;
    hierarchy: number;
    typography: number;
    color: number;
    spacing: number;
    imagery: number;
    effects: number;
    communication: number;
    technical: number;
  };

  qualityLevel: string;

  criticalFailures: string[];

  strengths: string[];

  weaknesses: string[];

  improvementPlan: {
    priority: "critical" | "high" | "medium" | "low";
    affectedElementIds: string[];
    problem: string;
    recommendedCorrection: string;
  }[];

  deliveryStatus: "approved" | "revision_required" | "rejected";
}
```

The category scores must use the exact maximum weights defined in Section 2.

The total score must be calculated from those weighted category scores, not invented independently.

# 8. TARGETED REVISION SYSTEM

The AI must correct weaknesses without unnecessarily destroying successful design decisions.

## Revision Priority

### Critical
Problems that make the design unusable.

Examples:
- Essential text missing.
- Broken rendering.
- Incorrect dimensions.
- Essential content obscured.

### High
Major visual weaknesses.

Examples:
- Weak composition.
- Poor hierarchy.
- Unreadable headline.
- Serious color contrast issues.

### Medium
Noticeable refinement opportunities.

Examples:
- Uneven spacing.
- Weak image integration.
- Poor typography pairing.
- Unbalanced decorative elements.

### Low
Minor artistic refinements.

Examples:
- Slight optical alignment.
- Subtle texture adjustment.
- Minor spacing improvement.

## Revision Rules

1. Correct critical failures first.
2. Correct high-priority problems next.
3. Preserve successful design decisions.
4. Modify only affected elements whenever possible.
5. Re-render after modifications.
6. Recalculate the quality score.
7. Stop when acceptance conditions are satisfied.

Use a configurable revision budget.

Recommended initial maximum: 3 automated revision rounds.

If the design still fails, present it for user review rather than entering an endless revision loop.

# 9. DESIGN CATEGORY-SPECIFIC EVALUATION

Different design categories must use different evaluation priorities.

The default 100-point scoring system remains the foundation, but the evaluator should adapt its interpretation to the design type.

## Poster

Prioritize:

- Visual impact.
- Main headline.
- Focal point.
- Composition.
- Readability.

## Banner

Prioritize:

- Immediate message recognition.
- Horizontal composition.
- Brand visibility.
- CTA prominence.
- Readability at reduced size.

## Flyer

Prioritize:

- Information organization.
- Headline hierarchy.
- Readability.
- Content grouping.
- Contact information.

## Brochure

Prioritize:

- Editorial consistency.
- Typography.
- Information structure.
- Multi-page continuity.
- Image and text relationships.

## Business Card

Prioritize:

- Brand identity.
- Typography.
- Alignment.
- Information accuracy.
- Print precision.

## Social Media Graphic

Prioritize:

- Immediate attention.
- Mobile readability.
- Visual identity.
- Platform-specific dimensions.
- Content clarity.

## Magazine Cover

Prioritize:

- Editorial art direction.
- Expressive typography.
- Image treatment.
- Layering.
- Cover hierarchy.

# 10. RESPONSIVE AND MULTI-SIZE QUALITY CHECKS

A design must be evaluated at more than one viewing scale.

Inspect:

- Full-resolution view.
- Normal viewing size.
- Reduced-size preview.
- Mobile-size preview where relevant.

Check:

- Headline readability.
- Essential text visibility.
- Image recognition.
- Visual hierarchy.
- Overall composition.

For multi-format designs, evaluate each adapted version independently.

Never assume that a design remains effective simply because it looked good in its original dimensions.

---

# 11. PRINT AND DIGITAL QUALITY REQUIREMENTS

## Digital Quality

Validate:

- Correct pixel dimensions.
- Appropriate resolution.
- Correct transparency.
- Color consistency.
- Image quality.
- Export integrity.

## Print Quality

Validate:

- Physical page dimensions.
- Appropriate raster resolution.
- Bleed.
- Safe margins.
- Crop marks when required.
- Font embedding or appropriate font conversion.
- Color profile.
- Image resolution.
- PDF output integrity.

Use 300 DPI as a common target for raster print artwork when appropriate, but determine actual requirements from document size and intended production process.

Do not use DPI alone as proof of print quality.

# 15. FINAL ACCEPTANCE POLICY

A design is approved only when:

- It satisfies the user's design brief.
- It preserves all essential content.
- It has no unresolved critical failures.
- It passes technical validation.
- It reaches the configured minimum quality score.
- It demonstrates appropriate artistic direction.
- It is visually coherent.
- It is suitable for its intended output format.

The default target is 90 or above, with 85 as the minimum delivery threshold.

A lower score must trigger revision or explicit user review.

No scoring system can perfectly measure artistic excellence. The score must support professional judgment, not replace it.

---

# FINAL PRINCIPLE

**A design that renders is not necessarily a good design.**

**A design that looks attractive is not necessarily a professional design.**

**A professional design must communicate effectively, demonstrate intentional artistic direction, maintain visual coherence, and satisfy technical production requirements.**

The purpose of this framework is to make quality evaluation an integral part of the design-generation process.

Do not simply generate.

Generate, inspect, measure, critique, refine, validate, and deliver.

**QUALITY IS NOT THE LAST STEP. QUALITY IS A REQUIREMENT AT EVERY STEP.**
